Viviana Dorado Estética — Rapid Website Research Dossier

Research scope: rapid public-source pass for a personalized website/demo. Findings below distinguish verified public evidence, public leads requiring confirmation, and website recommendations/inferences. Public information was concentrated on Instagram and Facebook; no reliable public Google rating, complete service menu, opening-hours listing, or standalone website was found in this pass.

Executive summary

Viviana Dorado Estética appears to be a Popayán, Cauca aesthetics and beauty business with an official Instagram profile, a Facebook page, and WhatsApp-led booking. Its public positioning combines integral aesthetics, beauty, body health, personalized attention, and cosmetology/cosmiatry expertise. The publicly visible offer spans facial care, body/wellness services, nails, and body-contouring or aesthetic services.

The strongest website opportunity is not to create a generic “spa” brochure. It is to turn a fragmented social-media presence into a clear local conversion path: show the current location, organize services by goal, explain what each treatment is for, surface real results and credentials, and send qualified inquiries directly to WhatsApp. The brand should feel warm, professional, personal, and results-oriented, rather than excessively clinical or unrelatedly high-fashion.

2. Brand and positioning

Verified public positioning

The Instagram bio describes the business as accredited as a “Técnico Laboral por Competencias en Cosmetología, Estética Integral, Cosmiatría, Marcación Abdominal.” [1] This is an important positioning asset: the business can present itself as more than a beauty-only service provider, while still avoiding unsupported medical claims.

A public Instagram post describes the business as “una estética integral de belleza y salud corporal” and links customers to WhatsApp. [5] A Facebook post says the business focuses on each patient’s wellbeing, recovery, personalized attention, and details intended to support optimal, lasting results. [6]

Likely target customers — inference

The public content suggests several overlapping audiences: local women and men seeking routine beauty services; clients seeking facial care; clients interested in body contouring or body wellness; and customers looking for convenient, personalized attention. The business also publicly mentioned service at home and in the consulting room, which could appeal to clients who value convenience or privacy. [7]

This is an inference from the service language and should be validated with Viviana before being used as formal brand strategy.

Brand personality and tone

The visible communication uses Spanish, emojis, direct calls to action, seasonal promotions, and reassuring wellness language. Repeated themes include feeling good, personalized care, wellbeing, recovery, beauty, and a “best version” promise. [5] [6] [8]

The tone is friendly, encouraging, feminine-leaning but not exclusively so, and promotional. A website can refine this into a more polished tone without losing warmth: calm, personal, expert, and locally approachable.

Visual direction from available evidence

The rapid text extraction did not reliably expose a full logo system or exact color values. The social presentation appears beauty/wellness-oriented and uses decorative emojis and treatment imagery. Do not impose a new unrelated luxury identity. A safe direction is:

  • warm neutrals or soft blush tones as a base;
  • one deeper accent such as cocoa, plum, terracotta, or muted green only after checking the actual logo/profile imagery;
  • generous whitespace and close-up treatment photography;
  • rounded but restrained UI details rather than a heavily clinical layout;
  • real people, hands, skin, nails, treatment rooms, and results as the main visual language.

These are website recommendations, not verified existing brand colors.

Customer needs the website should address — evidence-based inference

The public offer creates predictable questions that a website can answer before WhatsApp:

  • What is included in each facial, body, massage, or nail service?
  • Which treatment is appropriate for the customer’s goal?
  • Is the service available at home, in the consulting room, or both?
  • Where exactly is the new/current location?
  • What is the duration and price?
  • Is prior assessment required?
  • Which WhatsApp number is current?
  • What preparation, aftercare, contraindications, or maintenance are relevant?

These questions are inferred from the fragmented public information, not quoted as customer complaints.

9. Website opportunities

Highest-impact opportunities

  1. 1. Turn WhatsApp into a qualified booking funnel. Use service-specific buttons such as “Quiero reservar limpieza facial” instead of one generic contact button. Pre-fill the treatment name and ask for preferred date, service location, and whether the client is new.
  2. 2. Organize services by customer goal. “Quiero mejorar mi piel,” “Quiero relajarme,” “Quiero arreglar mis uñas,” and “Quiero trabajar una zona corporal” are easier for customers than an unexplained list of technical terms.
  3. 3. Clarify the current location and service modes. Display Calle 8 #10-57, barrio San Camilo, next to the Palacio de Justicia only after confirmation, plus a map, landmark, parking/access notes, and whether home service is available by zone.
  4. 4. Explain treatments, not just names. Each service should show who it is for, what happens, duration, price or quote logic, preparation, aftercare, and a WhatsApp CTA.
  5. 5. Make credentials visible. The cosmetology, integral-aesthetics, cosmiatry, and abdominal-marking credentials are a key trust asset. Ask for exact institution, certificate wording, and dates before publishing.
  6. 6. Build a consented results gallery. Use before/after or treatment-progress content with dates, treatment type, and a clear disclaimer that results vary.
  7. 7. Add trust elements that social media currently fragments. Testimonials, hygiene practices, service mode, FAQs, payment methods, cancellation policy, and clear contact details should live in one place.

10. Recommended website content

Homepage

  • Hero: a concise promise around personalized integral aesthetics and wellbeing in Popayán.
  • Primary CTA: Reservar por WhatsApp.
  • Secondary CTA: Ver tratamientos.
  • Three or four featured pathways: facial care, body/wellness, nails, and home/consulting-room attention if confirmed.
  • Short credentials block.
  • Selected result/gallery content.
  • Current location and hours.
  • Testimonials once consented and verified.

Services page

Create categories for facial, body/wellness, nails, and other aesthetic services. Each treatment needs a plain-language explanation, expected duration, price or “valoración/quote,” and a booking button.

About / trust page

Present Viviana’s professional identity, certifications, service philosophy, personalized attention, and hygiene standards. Avoid medical-sounding claims unless supported by credentials and regulatory requirements.

Promotions page or section

Use current promotions as time-limited cards with expiry dates. Do not leave old seasonal campaigns looking current.

Results and testimonials

Use approved client media, treatment context, and consent. Include text testimonials only when the customer has authorized publication.

FAQ

Answer booking, location, home service, preparation, contraindications, duration, payment, cancellation, and maintenance questions.

Contact page

Show the confirmed WhatsApp, any confirmed secondary phone, Instagram, Facebook, map, address, hours, and a short inquiry form that routes to WhatsApp or email if an email is later confirmed.

11. Visual direction

The site should feel personalized, calm, polished, and sensorial, but not like a generic luxury hotel spa or a medical clinic. Preserve the social brand’s warmth and Spanish-first communication. Recommended direction:

  • soft neutral base with a restrained blush/warm accent palette after checking the actual logo;
  • elegant serif for selected headings paired with a highly readable sans-serif for service details;
  • close-up skin, nails, hands, treatment textures, and welcoming interior photography;
  • rounded cards and generous spacing, with limited decorative effects;
  • real service photography prioritized over stock images;
  • mobile-first layout because Instagram and WhatsApp are the current discovery and booking paths;
  • prominent but tasteful WhatsApp buttons throughout.
